Meet Sophia Tung

I am a high school student (GPA: 3.85) who thrives on hands-on involvement, community service, and creative expression. Whether I am coordinating a multi-school medical convention, spending quality time visiting hospice patients, or illustrating children's books and creating art, I bring energy, leadership, and care to everything I take on.

  • Girl Scout Silver Award: Girl Scouts of the USA (2024)
  • Girl Scout Bronze Award: Girl Scouts of the USA (2022)
  • Certifications: Health & Safety Institute CPR & AED Trained (July 2026)
  • Media & Digital Tools: Toon Boom Harmony, Adobe After Effects, Adobe Premiere, CapCut, FlipaClip

My personal experience with dementia is through my Grandma, or 婆婆 (Popo) in Chinese. My Grandma was diagnosed with dementia in early 2021. It soon became obvious how the disease affects a person's memory, cognitive function, and fine motor skills. It started out with simple slip-ups, but over time it became more and more noticeable. I still remember getting a phone call from my Grandpa, who said that my Grandma was supposed to get groceries like usual, but somehow ended up in Kapolei. It seemed funny at the time, but I wish I had taken action earlier when I first noticed the signs—now she can barely remember that I am her granddaughter.

"I put small memorable pictures of her favorite trips to Macau, custom coloring sheets I made specifically for her, and tactile fidgets; she absolutely loved it! My grandma loved telling me about the pictures and coloring alongside me while I did my homework."

Recently, in late 2025, I began researching dementia activity kits specifically designed to help slow down cognitive decline. My family and I put together a kit for my Grandma in a small shoebox, and that is where it all started. Small connections like this are what fuel our brand.

Meet Emily Tomasso

I watch grandparents go to a store they have visited for years and suddenly forget where it is. That is why I am building a kit full of games and tools to help older adults keep their minds active and slow down memory loss.

Medical research shows that playing memory games helps keep brains sharp over time. I am taking that concept and putting it into a simple, easy-to-use kit that families can use at home with their older relatives.

I am currently a student at Hawaii Baptist Academy and part of the Blue Academy bootcamp in Honolulu. This week, I am turning this idea into an actual product instead of just thinking about it.

Before building this kit, I learned the value of working through hard problems by teaching my friends math when they got stuck and learning to ask for help when my chemistry homework confused me.
